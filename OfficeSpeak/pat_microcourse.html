The idea

A team of specialists.
That never clocks out.

We call it an office. Each worker has one job and a name. Information comes in through a source, gets passed worker to worker, and results go out through a sink — a file, an email, a screen.

The example

Here's a real one,
start to finish.

"Once each period my club gets a batch of market data, forecasts, and news, plus our decisions from last period. Two analysts each read it and recommend a plan — one value-investing, one chasing emerging opportunities. A manager weighs both, proposes a plan, checks the taxes and fees with an accountant, then writes the final plan to a file."

Step 1

You just describe it.

claude.ai — OfficeSpeak project
"Once each period my club gets a batch of market data... two analysts recommend a plan... a manager checks the cost with an accountant... writes the final plan to a file."
assistant is building your team

No special words or technical terms. Just say it the way you'd tell a person.

Step 2

It builds the team.

Each period lets one batch in. Both analysts read it and recommend a plan; the manager weighs both, checks the cost with the accountant, and writes the final plan down.

The important part

It tells you what it assumed.

—The two analysts are treated equally, and the manager has the final say.
—The office handles one period fully before starting the next, so the shared portfolio stays consistent.
—The accountant works out the taxes from the proposed plan alone — it does not look at what the club currently holds.

That third one is a real gap. This is exactly what to look for.

Step 3

You correct it.
In plain English.

what you type back
"The accountant has to see what we currently hold — otherwise the tax numbers are guesses. Taxes depend on what we paid for what we own."

No need to say it perfectly, or use the right words. Just react to what's in front of you.

Step 4

It shows you exactly what changed.

"One new link: before it prices anything, the accountant now looks up what the club currently holds and what we paid for it, and uses that for the tax math. Nothing else about the office changes."

The whole idea

Describe. Explain.
Correct. Repeat.

You describe
It explains
You correct

You never have to get it perfect up front. It's easier to react to a real team in front of you than to describe a correct one from nothing.

Later, optional

When it's right,
it can actually run.

Once you're happy with the team and its explanation, Stage 2 turns it into a real, running system — no guessing, nothing hidden: every piece gets checked against real examples before it's trusted to run on its own. For our investment club, that really produced numbers: period 1 → buy 8 shares, fee $8.00, period 2 → buy 16 shares, fee $16.80, and so on — the fee correctly growing from what the club already held, exactly as you asked.

You don't have to do this part yourself — right now, that just means sending it to us.
